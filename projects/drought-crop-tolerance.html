When the rain fails, which crops endure?

A national geospatial machine-learning study investigating how sixteen climatic and geographic drought factors shape the suitability and resilience of Aman rice, Aush rice, Boro rice, jute and wheat across Bangladesh.

01 · The Climate Problem

Drought is no longer a seasonal inconvenience. It is a food-security variable.

A climate-sensitive food system

Bangladesh’s major crops depend on seasonal rainfall, soil moisture, surface energy and irrigation. Increasing drought severity threatens both the area that can be cultivated and the productivity of land already under agriculture.

Sixteen interacting pressures

Drought cannot be understood through rainfall alone. Water deficit, evapotranspiration, soil moisture, radiation, vapour pressure, terrain, drainage and soil conditions interact non-linearly across the landscape.

The explainability challenge

Predicting crop suitability is only part of the task. Agricultural planning also requires a defensible explanation of which variables drive each crop’s response and how those influences change between suitability classes.

Can five major crops be evaluated spatially — and their drought tolerance explained — using a single interpretable machine-learning framework?

04 · Analytical Framework

From satellite-derived drought layers to interpretable crop decisions.

STEP 01

Compile crop suitability data

Suitability classifications for Aman rice, Aush rice, Boro rice, wheat and jute were assembled for use as the model outputs.

STEP 02

Acquire environmental layers in Google Earth Engine

Climatic datasets were filtered by national boundary, time period and image band using the Google Earth Engine JavaScript API.

STEP 03

Map and harmonise raster data

Exported TIF layers were processed in ArcMap alongside soil, elevation, slope, aspect and drainage information to create a consistent geospatial database.

STEP 04

Extract values at upazila scale

Values from the sixteen environmental layers were extracted for each upazila and combined with the crop-suitability classes in a structured analytical table.

STEP 05

Train and tune the Random Forest

The classifier was tuned with GridSearchCV. The manuscript reports 200 estimators, maximum depth 20, minimum samples per leaf 1 and minimum samples per split 2.

STEP 06

Explain predictions with SHAP

SHAP TreeExplainer was used to quantify how each environmental factor influenced the five suitability outcomes: very suitable, suitable, moderately suitable, marginally suitable and not suitable.

05 · Explainable Machine Learning

Prediction identifies the pattern. SHAP explains the pressure behind it.

Random Forest captures non-linear environmental interactions without assuming a simple relationship between drought and crop suitability. SHAP then translates those predictions into crop-specific explanations that can be interpreted by planners, agronomists and policymakers.

Interpretation note: drought tolerance is inferred from modelled suitability and environmental response patterns. It was not measured through controlled physiological crop-stress experiments.

Random Forest classifier

An ensemble of decision trees classifies crop suitability while reducing dependence on any single tree and accommodating complex relationships between climatic and geographic predictors.

07 · Drought Geography

The environmental layers reveal four different Bangladeshes.

Dry northwest conditions, humid coastal zones, wet eastern terrain and the elevated Chittagong Hill Tracts produce distinct combinations of water stress, atmospheric demand and topographic constraint.

Water demand & drought severity

AET, climate water deficit, PDSI and reference evapotranspiration describe water use, unmet demand and drought intensity.

Rainfall, runoff & atmospheric dryness

Rainfall and runoff show water supply and loss, while vapour pressure metrics describe atmospheric moisture and evaporative demand.

Root-zone and surface-energy conditions

Soil moisture and soil type describe the rooting environment; radiation and wind shape surface energy and crop water loss.

Terrain & drainage constraints

Elevation, slope, aspect and drainage density explain how terrain redistributes water, radiation and agricultural accessibility.

08 · Principal Findings

The most resilient crop is not the least water-dependent one.

01

Wheat shows the strongest drought tolerance

SHAP interpretation identifies wheat as the most drought-tolerant crop in the study, with elevation and surface-radiation dependence contributing to its response.

02

Climate water deficit dominates wheat

Climate water deficit records a reported feature-importance score of 0.56 for wheat, the strongest highlighted crop–factor relationship in the manuscript.

03

Aman rice is highly deficit-sensitive

Climate water deficit is the leading reported factor for Aman rice with a feature-importance score of 0.54. PDSI and slope also shape its suitability outcomes.

04

Soil moisture governs Aush rice

Soil moisture reaches a reported importance score of 0.57 for Aush rice. SHAP also associates its moderate tolerance with water deficit, PDSI and slope.

05

Jute depends strongly on root-zone water

Soil moisture is the leading highlighted factor for jute with a score of 0.56. The manuscript describes jute as comparatively less drought-tolerant than several other crops.

06

Boro rice responds to atmospheric moisture stress

Vapour-pressure conditions are central to Boro rice, with vapour pressure deficit highlighted at 0.57. Its overall drought response is characterised as moderate.

09 · Planning Relevance

A crop map becomes useful when it changes a land-use decision.

PLANNING 01

Match crops to drought conditions

Suitability maps can support crop-selection strategies that respond to local water deficit, soil moisture, atmospheric demand and terrain rather than applying one national recommendation.

PLANNING 02

Target water-management investment

Areas where climate water deficit, vapour-pressure deficit or soil moisture dominate can be prioritised for irrigation, water retention and drought-management interventions.

PLANNING 03

Protect food security under climate pressure

An explainable national framework helps policymakers distinguish crops and locations that require adaptation from those that retain stronger resilience under dry conditions.
