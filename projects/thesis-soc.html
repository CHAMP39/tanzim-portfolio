Predicting soil carbon from space.

A machine learning framework integrating Sentinel-1 SAR, Sentinel-2 optical imagery, topographic indices and climate covariates to map topsoil SOC at 10 m resolution across 4,394 km² of coastal Bangladesh — without a single additional field sample.

01 · Why This Matters

Soil carbon is invisible. We made it mappable.

The Carbon Crisis

SOC stores ~1,500 Gt globally — more than atmosphere + vegetation combined. Its depletion accelerates climate change. Khulna's deltaic soils are a critical but unmapped reservoir.

The Data Gap

Bangladesh has no high-resolution SOC map. Conventional field sampling costs $1,000s per km² and still misses fine-scale variability across tidal floodplains, polders, and mangroves.

The Hypothesis

Sentinel-1 radar + Sentinel-2 optical + terrain + climate, fed into ensemble ML, can predict SOC at 10 m resolution with >80% explained variance — at near-zero marginal cost per pixel.

"Higher SOC in southern coastal upazilas. Lower values in inland agricultural zones. The delta speaks — if you know how to listen."

04 · Analytical Framework

From satellite pixel to soil carbon prediction.

01

Data Acquisition

Sentinel-2 (COPERNICUS/S2_HARMONIZED), Sentinel-1 GRD (dry season Nov–Feb median), SRTM DEM (USGS/SRTMGL1_003), ERA5-Land climate (2001–2023 mean). All via Google Earth Engine at 10 m resolution.

02

Variable Selection

Pearson correlation (p < 0.05) removed non-significant variables. VIF > 10 removed SAVI (42.20), SWIR1 (381.90), VH (153.00). Correlation matrix screened pairs with r > 0.80. Final: 9 predictors retained.

03

ML Pipeline Construction

Scikit-learn pipelines with median imputation → StandardScaler for scale-sensitive models (SVR, KNN, MLP). Five algorithms: RF, GB (histogram-based), SVR (RBF kernel), MLP, KNN. Unified structure ensures no data leakage.

04

Model Training & Validation

5-fold cross-validation. Metrics: R², RMSE, MAE. Composite score = ⅓(1/RMSE + 1/MAE + R²) for holistic ranking. SHAP values computed for RF to explain feature-level influence.

05

Sensitivity Analysis

Perturbation-based: each predictor shifted ±10%. Performance deltas (ΔRMSE, ΔMAE, ΔR²) quantify each model's robustness to input noise. Identifies critical vs. redundant covariates per algorithm.

06

Spatial Prediction & Validation

Best model (RF) applied wall-to-wall across Khulna at 10 m. Moran's I test (I = 0.1447, p = 0.000259, z = 3.653) confirmed significant spatial clustering — validating spatial coherence of predictions.

06 · What Drives Soil Carbon?

Slope and vegetation dominate. Climate is secondary.

Feature importance across all five models consistently identified Slope (RF: 0.361, GB: 0.409) and NDVI (RF: 0.296, GB: 0.222) as dominant predictors. This aligns with the classical clorpt framework — relief and organisms drive SOC at local scales where macro-climate is uniform.

Topography dominates

Slope is #1 in 4 of 5 models. Even in Khulna's near-flat delta, micro-slope differences of <1° determine runoff, erosion, and organic matter retention.

Vegetation is the carbon pump

NDVI ranks #1 in MLP, #2 in RF and GB. Higher canopy cover = more litterfall, root turnover, and SOC input — confirming organisms as a key clorpt factor.

Radar adds what optics misses

Sentinel-1 VV ranked #3 in RF. Cloud-penetrating radar captures surface moisture and structural roughness where Sentinel-2 has monsoon cloud gaps.

09 · Model Robustness

What happens when the data is wrong? RF barely flinches.

Perturbation-based sensitivity analysis shifted each predictor by ±10% and measured ΔRMSE. This tests whether models remain reliable in real-world deployment where satellite data has noise, cloud contamination, or temporal mismatch.

Random Forest

🟢 MOST STABLE

ΔRMSE < ±0.07 for most predictors. NDVI −10% raises RMSE by only +0.21. Slope and TRI perturbations cause <0.07 shift. Recommended for operational deployment.

Gradient Boosting

🟡 STABLE OVERALL

NDVI −10% raises RMSE by +0.27 — highest among ensemble models. SLOPE −10% raises RMSE by +0.14. Use RF for robustness, GB for accuracy cross-check.

SVM

🟡 CLIMATE-SENSITIVE

TEMPERATURE ±10% causes ΔRMSE = ±1.56 — a critical failure mode. PRECIPITATION perturbations also destabilizing (+0.38/+0.43). Avoid for operational use.

MLP

🔴 CRITICALLY UNSTABLE

TEMPERATURE ±10% causes ΔRMSE = +41.5 (catastrophic). PRECIPITATION −10% raises RMSE by +0.85. Neural network sensitivity unresolved with 201 samples.

KNN

🔴 HIGHLY SENSITIVE

TEMPERATURE −10% causes ΔRMSE = +0.72. PRECIPITATION −10% raises RMSE by +0.35. Distance-based methods highly unstable under feature noise.

10 · Spatial Validation

The predictions cluster where they should. Moran's I confirms it.

Moran's I = 0.1447Significant positive spatial autocorrelation
z-score = 3.653Highly significant (p < 0.01)
p-value = 0.000259Clustering is not random

SOC values in Khulna are spatially clustered — high SOC zones neighbour high SOC zones. This validates that RF predictions are physically coherent with landscape structure, not random noise.

This is the study's spatial robustness proof. Most undergraduate SOC studies report only R² and RMSE. Adding Moran's I spatial autocorrelation testing places this work at the methodological level of peer-reviewed digital soil mapping literature.
11 · Findings & Implications

What this means for Bangladesh — and for the planet.

01

Random Forest wins

RF (R²=0.81) outperforms GB, SVM, MLP and KNN. Ensemble tree methods are optimal for SOC prediction with moderate sample sizes and correlated RS covariates.

02

Slope + NDVI are universal controls

Topography and vegetation dominate in 4 of 5 models. Even in a near-flat delta, micro-relief determines SOC fate. Organisms and relief are the dominant clorpt factors at this scale.

03

Sentinel-1 SAR adds irreplaceable value

VV backscatter ranked 3rd in RF. Radar penetrates cloud cover ubiquitous in Bangladesh's monsoon climate, capturing soil moisture and roughness unavailable to optical sensors.

04

A clear south–north carbon gradient

Koyra (coast, mangrove, ~9% SOC) to Paikgachha (inland, agriculture, ~3% SOC). Protecting coastal upazilas is protecting carbon.

05

Croplands are SOC sinks

Mean SOC in crops (4.88%) is 41% lower than in forests (8.35%). Carbon-smart agriculture — cover crops, residue retention — could add measurable SOC stocks.

06

First fine-scale SOC map of Khulna

No prior study produced a 10 m resolution, wall-to-wall SOC surface for this region. This is a baseline for carbon accounting, soil health monitoring, and climate adaptation planning.

Policy implications

  • This map can directly support Bangladesh's Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC) to the Paris Agreement — providing spatially explicit soil carbon stock estimates per hectare.
  • Identifies high-SOC zones (Koyra, Dacope mangroves) for immediate conservation priority.
  • Identifies SOC-depleted zones (Paikgachha, Dumuria croplands) for targeted carbon farming intervention.
12 · Limitations & Future Directions

What this study does not yet solve.

Limitations

  • Restricted to 0–20 cm topsoil. Subsoil SOC (which can exceed surface stocks in some deltaic soils) not modeled.
  • 201 samples over 4,394 km² (~4.6 pts/100 km²). Fine-scale heterogeneity in polders and mangrove margins may be underrepresented.
  • Intrinsic soil properties (texture, mineralogy, parent material) excluded — no SRDI laboratory data available for these variables.
  • Temporal snapshot only. SOC dynamics under seasonal flooding and cyclone events not captured.
  • No formal uncertainty quantification (prediction intervals, quantile forests) — scope limitation.

Future directions

  • Multi-depth sampling (0–20, 20–50, 50–100 cm) to estimate full profile SOC stocks for carbon accounting.
  • Time-series Sentinel data — seasonal NDVI composites, flood-period SAR — to capture temporal SOC dynamics.
  • Integration of soil texture and mineralogy from laboratory data to improve model parsimony.
  • Transferability testing to adjacent coastal districts (Satkhira, Bagerhat) and pan-Bangladesh SOC mapping.
  • Quantile RF or Gaussian Process models for uncertainty mapping — producing confidence envelopes around SOC predictions.
  • Deep learning approaches (CNN on image patches, Graph Neural Networks for spatial autocorrelation) for next-generation accuracy.
